What is
your clan?

A speculative survey of twenty patrilines and matrilines that may or may not still travel through living veins.

Most of what you inherit is shuffled at every generation. But two pieces refuse to mix. The Y-chromosome passes father to son, unchanged. Mitochondrial DNA passes mother to daughter, unchanged. Each is a kind of receipt, a name written in nucleotides that survives, sometimes, for thousands of years.

This survey asks a simple, slightly absurd question. Of the twenty famous figures catalogued here, whose receipt are you most likely carrying? Answer three questions about your heritage and the survey will name your most plausible clan.

On the method

The Y-chromosome makes up roughly 0.9% of a man's genome, about 57 million base pairs, and passes father to son with almost no mixing. After eighty generations, it is still a 99.99998% match to the original. Mitochondrial DNA does the same for the mother-line. Everything else recombines.

The survey is a Fermi-style toy, not a test. Real Y-DNA testing through FTDNA Big-Y or YFull is the only way to actually know your haplogroup.
